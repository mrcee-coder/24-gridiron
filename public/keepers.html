THE KEEPER
LEDGER.

One keeper. Three keeper seasons. A draft bargain worth finding.

Keeper rules · updated November 12, 2026

One keeper per team. Players originally drafted in Round 5 or later, including Round 5, are eligible. The first keeper season costs one round earlier than the original draft round. Each additional keeper season costs one round earlier than the previous keeper cost. A Round 12 pick costs Round 11 next year, then Round 10 the following year. Current rankings and ADP do not change that price.

Maximum three keeper seasons after the original draft season. The original draft season does not count toward the limit. Keeper seasons accumulate across all managers. After the third keeper season, the player returns to the draft pool for the following season. Releasing or trading a player does not restart this clock.

The keeper cost and keeper-season history follow the player through trades, drops, waiver claims and free-agent pickups; those moves reset neither. Undrafted players remain outside the drafted-Round-5+ rule.

Waiver ruling · November 12, 2026

Keeper eligibility follows the player’s original draft round through trades, drops, waiver claims and free-agent pickups. A player originally drafted in Round 5 or later retains that draft qualification and the same keeper cost progression, subject to the three-keeper-season maximum. Players drafted in Rounds 1–4 remain ineligible even after being dropped. Undrafted players do not qualify under the drafted-Round-5+ rule.

Priority groups use original draft investment (Rounds 5–10), current depth-chart role, and injury reserve status. They are browsing aids, not rest-of-season or 2027 rankings. Injury labels come from Sleeper and may lag NFL news.
